The Tackle Stick system puts the outcome of every defensive stop directly in your hands. Instead of relying on automatic wrap animations, you flick the right stick in one of four directions to choose your tackle type — and then commit to perfect timing to execute it. Get it right and you force fumbles, drop ball carriers for losses, and shut down drives. Get it wrong and you whiff into open field, giving up extra yards or a touchdown. Understanding which tackle to use, when to use it, and how to time your input is the difference between a shutdown defense and a liability.

## Directional Flick Reference

Each right-stick flick maps to a distinct tackle type with its own ideal matchup and risk profile. Study this table before you step onto the field.

| Flick Direction | Tackle Type | Best Use Case | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Up** | High Tackle / Truck Attempt | Stopping upright runners, forcing fumbles on big hits | High — easily beaten by spin, juke, or hurdle; high fumble force on contact |
| **Down** | Low Wrap / Gator Roll | Bringing down power backs, stuffing inside runs | Vulnerable to hurdles; leaves you exposed if the back leaps over your dive |
| **Left** | Wrap Angle Correction Left | Re-aligning your approach on a ball carrier cutting left | Low — purely corrective; prevents suction-tackle animations from misfiring |
| **Right** | Wrap Angle Correction Right | Re-aligning your approach on a ball carrier cutting right | Low — purely corrective; prevents suction-tackle animations from misfiring |

## Timing Windows

Flicking the right stick at the correct moment is just as important as picking the right direction. The game evaluates your input relative to your defender's proximity to the ball carrier and assigns one of three timing outcomes.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Perfect Timing">
    Your flick lands in the optimal window — your defender fires into the correct tackle animation with full force. Fumble probability peaks on High Tackles, and Low Wraps secure the ball carrier with no chance of escape.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Early Trigger">
    You flick before your defender is close enough to make authoritative contact. The result is a glancing blow — the ball carrier absorbs reduced force and has a chance to break free or spin out of the tackle.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Late Trigger">
    You flick after the ideal window has closed. Your defender over-runs the ball carrier or reaches with an arm tackle, producing a missed tackle or a weak hold that a strong back can power through.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Avoid overcommitting to the High Tackle (flick up) against elusive backs — shifty runners, speed receivers, and dual-threat quarterbacks will punish you with a spin or juke that leaves your defender completely out of position. Reserve the High Tackle for power backs running upright into contact or tight-window situations where the ball carrier has nowhere to redirect.
</Warning>

## Situational Tackle Guide

Different game situations call for different tackle inputs. The sections below break down the most common scenarios you'll face.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Stopping Power Backs">
    Against fullbacks, heavy running backs, or any ball carrier with high trucking ratings, the **Low Wrap / Gator Roll** (flick down) is your safest option. It cuts the legs out from under the runner before momentum can build, negating the power advantage entirely. Time it slightly early rather than late — an early low wrap still clips legs, while a late one misses behind the runner's stride.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="High-Speed Perimeter Plays">
    On jet sweeps, wide receiver end-arounds, and open-field perimeter runs, your priority is getting your defender's body angle right before committing to a tackle type. Use **Wrap Angle Correction** (flick left or right, matching the ball carrier's cut direction) to snap your defender into a proper pursuit path, then follow immediately with a Low Wrap or neutral-stick wrap once you're in range.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Forcing Fumbles">
    The **High Tackle** (flick up) carries the highest fumble-force value in the game, but it demands precise timing and an upright ball carrier. Look for runners who have absorbed contact and are already slowing down, or running backs with low carry ratings fighting through a pile. A perfectly timed High Tackle in those moments can swing possession — and field position — instantly.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Goal Line Stands">
    Inside the five-yard line, keep the right stick neutral unless you have a clear angle on the ball carrier. The short field compresses pursuit lanes and a missed flick leaves a gap for an easy score. Let your defensive line's power ratings do the work — save the directional inputs for the second-level defenders who have a clear view of the play developing.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Tackle Type Effectiveness by Ball Carrier

Not all tackles work equally against all runners. The game factors in ball carrier **weight**, **speed**, and **position** when calculating whether your tackle attempt succeeds.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="High Tackle (Flick Up)" icon="arrow-up">
    **Strong against:** Slow-developing inside runs, upright receivers after the catch, quarterbacks scrambling in the open field.

    **Weak against:** Elusive backs with high juke/spin ratings, receivers with strong break-tackle ratings, any ball carrier moving laterally at speed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Low Wrap (Flick Down)" icon="arrow-down">
    **Strong against:** Power backs, fullbacks, any ball carrier running upright and forward into the defense.

    **Weak against:** Backs with elite hurdle ratings, outside runs where the ball carrier has room to jump over a diving defender.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Wrap Angle Left (Flick Left)" icon="arrow-left">
    **Best used:** When your defender is approaching from the right and the ball carrier cuts left — corrects pursuit angle and cancels misfire animations.

    **Not a tackle on its own:** Always follow with a second input once your angle is corrected.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Wrap Angle Right (Flick Right)" icon="arrow-right">
    **Best used:** When your defender is approaching from the left and the ball carrier cuts right — corrects pursuit angle and cancels misfire animations.

    **Not a tackle on its own:** Always follow with a second input once your angle is corrected.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
  Use Wrap Angle Corrections proactively whenever you see the ball carrier begin a cut. These inputs prevent the engine from locking your defender into a suction-tackle animation — the automatic grab that pulls your player toward the ball carrier regardless of realistic approach geometry. Breaking out of that animation before it triggers keeps your defender athletic and responsive rather than statically committed to a bad angle.
</Tip>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

<Accordion title="Flicking too early on contact plays">
  The most common error in the Tackle Stick system is triggering a flick the moment you *see* the ball carrier rather than when your defender reaches tackling range. Early flicks produce glancing blows and break-tackle opportunities. Watch your defender's feet — when they're within one stride of the ball carrier, that is your window.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Ignoring ball carrier weight and position">
  Using the same flick direction regardless of who is carrying the ball is a fast path to missed tackles. A Low Wrap that floors a 250-pound fullback will fail against a 185-pound slot receiver who hops it effortlessly. Read the personnel on the field and adjust your inputs accordingly before the snap.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Overusing the High Tackle in space">
  The High Tackle is a finisher, not an opener. Using it in open field against elusive runners is a high-risk gamble that leaves your defender face-down while the ball carrier accelerates away. Build a containment angle first, then commit to the High Tackle only when you have leverage.
</Accordion>
